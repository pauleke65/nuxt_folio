Urban transit under a fuel shock

Twenty million trips a day across four modes sharing one scarce resource. Fares are the visible variable; road area per capita is the binding one. Built on a metropolitan case, then tested against two cities with different fare regimes.

Actors & incentives

Informal operators
Maximise daily cash; reprice within hours of a cost shock.
Transit authority
Fare-box recovery against a politically capped fare.
Commuters
Minimise cost plus time, weighted by reliability of arrival.
State
Visible capacity announcements over throughput per unit spent.
03

Leverage points

  1. Low

    Add vehicles to an existing corridor.

  2. Medium

    Change how passengers board and pay. Dwell time is the throughput cap.

  3. High

    Price road space at peak, so mode choice carries the true cost.

  4. Extreme

    Stop treating transport as the movement of vehicles and treat it as access to opportunity, which makes land use, not roads, the intervention.

04

What would prove me wrong

Falsifier

If a 15% fuel rise leaves ridership flat for two consecutive months, cost is not driving mode choice and the model’s central arrow is wrong.
